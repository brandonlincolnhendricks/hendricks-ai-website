How this works

You asked about putting AI agents to work in your restaurant: finding the best price from your suppliers, keeping track of what sells, and reordering automatically. All three can be built. How well they work depends on three things this questionnaire is designed to find out: where your sales, recipe and invoice information lives today, how ordering actually happens week to week, and how much you want an agent to do without checking with you first.

An agent that reorders on its own is only as good as the numbers behind it. It needs to know what sold, what each dish uses, what is on the shelf and what each supplier is charging this week. Where one of those is missing, the right first agent is the one that collects it, not the one that spends money. Your answers tell us which of those situations we are starting from.

Two notes before you start. First, best price is only as good as the prices an agent can see. Most foodservice distributors price by account and change prices often, so the agent learns your real prices from your invoices and order guides rather than from a public website. Section 4 asks where those live. Second, nothing here commits you to anything, and section 6 lets you decide exactly what an agent may do on its own.

Questions marked Priority matter most. If you only have ten minutes, answer those and leave the rest for our call. Your answers save as you type, both in this browser and to a private backup at Hendricks, so you can stop at any point and pick up later. To finish on another device, press Copy link on the bar at the bottom of the screen and open that link there. Leave anything blank you are unsure about.

04

Prices and suppliers, the best-price section

Why this section mattersThis section decides whether a price-comparison agent is worth building. It only works when it can see current prices for the same item from more than one supplier, and it pays off on the items you spend the most on, not on everything.
20Where do your prices live today?Priority

Check all that apply.

21Roughly how much do you spend on food, beverage and supplies?

Ballpark is fine. This tells us how much a better price is actually worth to you.

22Which ten to twenty items do you spend the most on?

In most kitchens a short list of items, usually proteins, fryer oil, cheese, produce staples and to-go packaging, drives most of the spend. Those are where price comparison pays. Brand and pack size help: "Tyson 8 oz chicken breast, 4 x 10 lb case" is far more useful than "chicken".

23Which items must never be switched, whatever the price?

The specs that make your food yours: a brand, a cut, a grade, a pack size or a supplier relationship you will not trade away. The agent treats these as locked.

24Would you split an order across suppliers to save money?

Splitting can save money on the item but cost it back in delivery minimums, fees and extra deliveries to check in.

25Do you have contract or deal pricing with any supplier, or belong to a buying group?
